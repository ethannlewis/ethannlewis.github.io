## Publications

**Peer Institution Networks, Test-Optional Admission Policies, and STEM Major Completions**  
*Journal of Economic Behavior and Organization*, 2026  
[Article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268126002611) | [Online Appendix](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/wfw8gazl4u6j5mmxp01n2/Online_Appendix.pdf?rlkey=53tyk6hk6hzhwpjsqxba6n7sk&st=7sygosf6&dl=0)

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<!-- Paste your published abstract here -->
I build a network of private colleges from their self-reported peer institutions and detect communities within it, comparing test-optional adopters to non-adopters within the same community. Adoption raises reported SAT scores but decreases the share of bachelor's degree completions in STEM. At the most selective liberal arts colleges, adoption reduces STEM completions by 8 percent and under-represented minority STEM completions by 26 percent.

## Working Papers

**Does Simplifying Master's Degree Admissions Increase Attainment? Evidence from Texas A&M University**  
with Aron Malatinszky  
*Draft available soon*

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<!-- Paste your abstract here -->
We study Quick Admit, a 2020 Texas A&M policy that streamlined applications and nearly guaranteed admission to engineering master's programs for seniors and alumni with GPAs above 3.0. Using a regression discontinuity design and Texas administrative data, we find that eligibility increases applications and enrollment for marginal students. Effects are largest for TAMU-specific outcomes, suggesting some substitution away from other programs, and are concentrated in the cohort graduating in Spring 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic.
</details>
